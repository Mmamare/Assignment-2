(1) Allocations fail in ./test 3 because the allocator suffers from external fragmentation. When blocks are 
freed, they are markeed as available but adjacent free blocks are not combined. Over many malloc/free operations, 
memory becomes divided into many small free blocks. Even when enough total free memory exists, there may not be
one contiguous free block largee enough for the requested allocation.

(2) The throughput plot shows that coalescing allocator generally performs as well as or better than the original 
allocator, especially for larger allocation sizes. For example, at 256vbytees, the coalescing version has a throughput
of about 16.3 million operations per second compared with about 10.4 million for the original version. Although coalescing adds some 
work during free(), it reduces fragmentation and allows memory to be reused more efficiently. As a result, feweer allocation attempts fail
and the allocator spends less time searching through fragmented free blocks.

(3) The coalescing allocator has a much better fail rate, especially as the maximum allocation size increases.
The original allocator's failure rate increases from about 10% at 32 bytes to about 63% at 256 bytes, while the coalescing allocator remains close
to 10% for all tested sizes. This happens because the original allocator leaves adjacent free blocks separated, causing external fragmentation. coalescing
combines neighboring free blocks into larger contiguous regions, making it much more likely that new allocation requests can find 
a sufficiently large free block.
